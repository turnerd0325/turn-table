Six rules that keep the focus on learning.

Every new Turn-Table game is built on all six. They're the difference between studying with a game and just playing one.

The science is the move

No points for clicking fast. Students win by placing, building, balancing or predicting with the actual idea.

Predict before you run

Students commit to a prediction first. Then the simulation shows whether the idea in their head matches what really happens.

Every miss is explained

A wrong answer gets a short, specific explanation of what went wrong, not just a red X.

Limited tries

Stars come from getting it right early. Guessing until something works doesn't pay.

Say it in your own words

Science Check questions make students type a full-sentence explanation before they move on. Hints walk them to the key idea.

Tied to a standard

Every game is built on a specific middle school standard, and the tag sits right on the game card.

Built on the study habit with the strongest research behind it.

It's called retrieval practice: pulling an idea out of memory and using it, instead of rereading it. Every Turn-Table level is retrieval practice.

61%vs 40%

A week later, students who practiced by testing themselves remembered 61% of a passage. Students who reread it remembered 40%.

Practiced recalling61%
Reread it40%
Roediger & Karpicke (2006), Psychological Science
50classroom experiments

A 2021 review looked only at real schools and classrooms. Most of the experiments (57%) found medium or large benefits from retrieval practice, across grade levels and subjects.

Agarwal, Nunes & Blunt (2021), Educational Psychology Review

The catch is that retrieval only helps when students actually have to think. A game that rewards tapping fast skips that part. Turn-Table makes the thinking the only way through.

Questions teachers ask

Is this just more screen time?

It's practice time on a screen. Each game is short, tied to a standard and built around retrieval practice. Use it for a 10-minute review or a Pre-Test, not a whole period of gaming. On a no-device day, run the live quiz or the review board on one projector.

What do students need?

A Chromebook or any device with a browser, your class code, a nickname and a 4-digit PIN. No email, no real name and nothing to install.

How is this different from the review games we already use?

Most review games ask a multiple-choice question and add a game around it. In Turn-Table there's no separate game part: placing the Moon, building the cell or wiring the circuit is the question. You can't win by recognizing a word fast.

What grade levels does it cover?

Middle school science, grades 6 to 8. Every game is tagged with the middle school standard it practices.

What happens when the trial ends?

If you continue, nothing changes. If you don't, your classes and scores stay, and your students can keep playing a free version: Speed Find, Cell Shop and Moonwatch, first levels, a few plays a day.
